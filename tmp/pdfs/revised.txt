     Certiﬁed Coarse-to-Fine Extraction of Token
                    Transducers
 A corrected framework for quotient graphs, reﬁnement, and residual replacement

                                  Revised formal synthesis

                                          31 July 2026


                                            Abstract
        We give a precise version of a coarse-to-ﬁne method for replacing the observable
    behavior of a deterministic token-processing system by an explicit graph. The central
    object is not a clustering of hidden vectors, but a quotient certiﬁcate: an encoding
    of reachable states for which observations and token transitions commute with graph
    operations. Such a certiﬁcate implies equality on every ﬁnite token trace. A tower of
    encodings is coherent when each coarse encoding factors through the next ﬁner one; this
    guarantees that reﬁnement never forgets an earlier distinction. We also separate two
    notions that are easily conﬂated: reﬁning an abstraction and transferring execution from
    a residual implementation to a graph. A patch may be transferred only on a domain
    where extensional equality has been proved, and complete parameter zeroing is justiﬁed
    only after the deployed evaluator no longer calls the residual. The exact results are proved
    for deterministic transducers; stochastic and approximate extensions are stated only with
    the additional hypotheses they require. Machine-checked Lean proofs accompany the core
    congruence, trace-exactness, reﬁnement, bisimulation, and patch-invariance claims.

1     Scope and the necessary distinctions
A token model can be treated operationally as a stateful transducer. Its state can be con-
tinuous and high dimensional while its interface consists of token-conditioned updates and
observations. An extraction claim must identify what is preserved:
    (i) trace behavior: observations after every allowed ﬁnite input word;

 (ii) reachable internal state: enough information to reconstruct the original hidden state;

 (iii) parameters: a particular numerical realization of the computation.
This paper targets (i). It neither requires (ii) nor implies (iii). Distinct neural parameteriza-
tions can implement the same transducer, and a behavioral quotient intentionally discards
hidden distinctions with no observable consequence.
    The exact theory below concerns deterministic, total, discrete-time transducers. The
hidden-state type and output type need not be ﬁnite. Finiteness enters only when claiming
that a ﬁnite graph exists or that a reﬁnement loop terminates. Claims about sampling,
continuous regions, or ﬂoating-point implementations require separate error or semantics
contracts.


2     Deterministic token transducers
Deﬁnition 2.1 (Transducer). A deterministic Moore-style token transducer is a tuple

                                  N = (H, Σ, Y, step, obs, h0 ),

where step : H × Σ → H, obs : H → Y , and h0 ∈ H. For w ∈ Σ∗ deﬁne

                       run(h, ϵ) = h,     run(h, aw) = run(step(h, a), w).

Its reachable set is HR = {run(h0 , w) : w ∈ Σ∗ }.
   The convention above consumes words from left to right. All quotient obligations may
be restricted to HR , provided successor closure of HR is used.
Deﬁnition 2.2 (Future behavioral equivalence). For h, h′ ∈ H, write

                 h ≡B h′    ⇐⇒     ∀w ∈ Σ∗ , obs(run(h, w)) = obs(run(h′ , w)).

Lemma 2.3 (Congruence). If h ≡B h′ , then step(h, a) ≡B step(h′ , a) for every a ∈ Σ.
Proof. Apply the hypothesis to the word aw for an arbitrary continuation w.

   Behavioral equivalence is therefore an equivalence relation compatible with every token
transition. The observation is constant on each class by taking the empty continuation.

3    Exact quotient graphs
Deﬁnition 3.1 (Functional quotient certiﬁcate). Let Q be a proposed graph-state set. A
certiﬁcate consists of

                        q : HR → Q,        λ : Q → Y,        δ :Q×Σ→Q

such that, for all h ∈ HR and a ∈ Σ,

                                       λ(q(h)) = obs(h),
                                                                                           (1)
                                     δ(q(h), a) = q(step(h, a)).

Only graph states in q(HR ) matter; arbitrary unreachable graph states can be removed.

    Extend δ to words by δ ∗ (r, ϵ) = r and δ ∗ (r, aw) = δ ∗ (δ(r, a), w).

Theorem 3.2 (Trace exactness). If (1) hold, then for every h ∈ HR and w ∈ Σ∗ ,

                                 λ(δ ∗ (q(h), w)) = obs(run(h, w)).

Proof. Induction on w. The empty case is the ﬁrst line of (1). In the token case, the second
line of (1) identiﬁes the next graph state with the encoding of the next concrete state, after
which the induction hypothesis applies.

Theorem 3.3 (Finite exact quotient criterion). There is a ﬁnite functional quotient certiﬁ-
cate on HR if and only if the restriction of ≡B to HR has ﬁnitely many equivalence classes.

Proof. If the behavioral quotient is ﬁnite, use its classes as Q; congruence makes δ well-
deﬁned and the empty-word case makes λ well-deﬁned. Conversely, if a ﬁnite certiﬁcate
q : HR → Q exists, equality under q implies behavioral equivalence by trace exactness. Thus
each ﬁber of q lies in one behavioral class, and the number of behavioral classes is at most
the number of nonempty ﬁbers, hence ﬁnite.

Remark 3.4. The converse relies on a functional state encoding satisfying the commuting
equations. Merely matching outputs from one initial state, without relating reachable con-
crete and graph states, is too weak to serve as this certiﬁcate.


4    Partitions and coherent reﬁnement towers
Represent a partition by a surjective encoding q : HR → Q. A second encoding q ′ : HR → Q′
is ﬁner when there is a forgetful map π : Q′ → Q with

                                             q = π ◦ q′.                                   (F)

Proposition 4.1 (No loss under reﬁnement). Under (F), q(x) ̸= q(y) implies q ′ (x) ̸= q ′ (y).

Proof. The contrapositive follows by applying π.

    A coherent tower therefore has the form
                      π0              π1
              Q0              Q1              Q2              ···       qk = πk ◦ qk+1 .

The equation is the substantive invariant. Calling independently trained clusterings a “hier-
archy” does not establish it.

5    Finite partition reﬁnement
Suppose HR , Σ, and the current abstract state set are ﬁnite. Given q : HR → Q, deﬁne the
signature                           (                          )
                          sigq (h) = obs(h), (q(step(h, a)))a∈Σ .
The next partition is the common reﬁnement of the current partition and equality of signa-
tures:
                     h ∼q+ h′ ⇐⇒ q(h) = q(h′ ) ∧ sigq (h) = sigq (h′ ).
Including q(h) = q(h′ ) is essential: it makes every round reﬁnement-only.

Deﬁnition 5.1 (Stable partition). A partition q is stable when observations are constant
on each ﬁber and, for every a, successor encodings are constant on each ﬁber. Equivalently,
there exist λ and δ satisfying (1).

Theorem 5.2 (Finite termination and coarsest stable reﬁnement). Starting from a partition
with m nonempty blocks on an n-element reachable set, repeated signature reﬁnement reaches
a stable partition after at most n − m proper rounds. Its limit is the coarsest stable partition
reﬁning the initial one.

Proof. A proper reﬁnement increases the number of nonempty blocks, which is bounded
by n. For minimality, let s be any stable reﬁnement of the initial partition. Inductively,
equality under s implies equality under every computed partition: stability of s supplies
equal observations and s-equivalent successors, while the induction hypothesis makes those
successors equal in the current partition. Hence s reﬁnes the limit.

   For a subset A ⊆ Σ, the same algorithm gives only A-stability. It is an exact graph for
the subalphabet A, not automatically for all tokens.


6    Counterexample-guided outer reﬁnement
When enumeration is expensive, candidate partitions may be reﬁned using a veriﬁed witness
(h, h′ , w) satisfying

                    q(h) = q(h′ )   and obs(run(h, w)) ̸= obs(run(h′ , w)).

Such a witness proves that the current partition cannot be an exact quotient. It may motivate
a predicate or token-class split, but the resulting partition must still be checked against the
commuting obligations.
   Failure to ﬁnd a witness has no logical force unless the search is complete for the declared
domain. Consequently an implementation should classify each level as one of:


status                  meaning
certiﬁed exact          universal quotient obligations proved on the stated reachable domain
certiﬁed approximate    an explicit metric, horizon, and uniform bound proved
empirical               only a ﬁnite sample or incomplete search was checked

These labels must not be interchanged.

7    Bisimulation as a relational certiﬁcate
A functional quotient is convenient but not mandatory.

Deﬁnition 7.1 (Bisimulation). For a graph (Q, δ, λ), a relation R ⊆ HR ×Q is a bisimulation
certiﬁcate when
                           (                                               )
               R(h, r) =⇒ obs(h) = λ(r) ∧ ∀a ∈ Σ, R(step(h, a), δ(r, a)) .

Theorem 7.2 (Bisimulation implies trace equivalence). If R(h, r), then for every w ∈ Σ∗ ,

                               obs(run(h, w)) = λ(δ ∗ (r, w)).

Proof. Induction on the word, using output agreement in the empty case and transition
closure before the induction hypothesis in the token case.

   For exact replacement, the initial pair must belong to R, and every transition used by
execution must be covered. A one-way simulation can establish some safety properties but
does not in general establish equality of outputs.


8    Certiﬁed residual replacement
Reﬁnement of state descriptions and transfer of execution are separate processes. Let X
be the deployed input domain and T : X → Z the original evaluator. At stage k, let
Mk : X → Z be the hybrid evaluator and Dk ⊆ X a proposed transfer domain.

Deﬁnition 8.1 (Patch certiﬁcate). A candidate Mk+1 is certiﬁed from Mk on Dk when

               ∀x ∈ Dk , Mk+1 (x) = T (x),       ∀x ∈
                                                    / Dk , Mk+1 (x) = Mk (x).

Theorem 8.2 (Patch invariance). If Mk = T extensionally and the two patch obligations
hold, then Mk+1 = T extensionally.

Proof. Split on membership in Dk .

    By induction, any ﬁnite sequence of certiﬁed patches preserves the original evaluator.
    This formulation avoids a type error present in informal accounts: a graph transition
usually returns an abstract state, whereas the neural step returns a hidden state. These
values should not be equated directly. Equality is instead asserted for a common externally
meaningful result type Z, or mediated by an explicit relation and decoder.

Corollary 8.3 (Safe residual removal). Suppose the ﬁnal deployed evaluator is proved equal
to T on its full declared domain and its execution path is independent of a residual module.
Replacing that unreachable module’s stored parameters by arbitrary values, including zero,
leaves the evaluator unchanged.

    This is a statement about the deployed dependency graph, not about mutual information
or small weight norms. It does not license zeroing shared embeddings, routing code, or
preprocessing still used by the graph implementation.

9     A sound extraction protocol
A practical exact protocol can now be stated without assuming that heuristic search is proof.
    1. Fix execution semantics, token alphabet, observation equality, and a reachable-state
       contract.

    2. Propose an initial encoding q0 and record whether it is merely empirical or already
       certiﬁed.

    3. Reﬁne by signatures or veriﬁed distinguishing continuations. Emit an explicit factor
       map πk for every accepted level.

    4. Propose graph transitions and outputs, then prove (1) or a relational bisimulation
       obligation.

    5. Transfer a residual execution region only after the patch obligations are proved.

    6. Stop with one of three honest products: a complete exact graph; a graph with a certiﬁed
       approximation contract; or a partial graph plus a precisely scoped residual.
Theorem 9.1 (Conditional completion for a ﬁnite explicit system). Assume HR and Σ
are ﬁnite, all reachable transitions can be enumerated, and the target graph language can
represent arbitrary ﬁnite deterministic graphs. Then signature reﬁnement terminates and
its stable quotient is exact. Enumerating and installing all of its transition and output rules
under the patch obligations yields a complete evaluator after ﬁnitely many transfers.
Proof. Termination and exactness follow from the ﬁnite reﬁnement theorem. The quotient
has ﬁnitely many state-token pairs. Each veriﬁed installation removes at least one uncovered
pair and never invalidates an installed rule; therefore all pairs are installed after ﬁnitely many
strict transfers.

    For continuous-state neural models, these hypotheses are generally unavailable. A claim
that “fair generation and complete veriﬁcation” forces algorithmic termination must addition-
ally deﬁne eﬀective fairness, ensure that every required predicate is expressible and eventually
generated, and ensure each veriﬁcation call terminates. Without these eﬀective assumptions
the statement is an idealized completeness speciﬁcation, not a termination guarantee.


10      Approximation contracts
Let (Y, dY ) be a metric output space. For a ﬁnite horizon L deﬁne
                                          (                                )
                     dL (h, h′ ) = max∗ dY obs(run(h, w)), obs(run(h′ , w)) ,
                                 w∈Σ
                                 |w|≤L

when the maximum exists; otherwise use a supremum. A block C is (L, ε)-coherent when
dL (h, h′ ) ≤ ε for all h, h′ ∈ C.
    This within-block property alone does not prove that a chosen graph representative ac-
cumulates no larger error under iterative graph execution. A certiﬁed approximate graph
should state the direct trace contract
                                              (                                )
                ∀h ∈ HR ∀w, |w| ≤ L : dY obs(run(h, w)), λ(δ ∗ (q(h), w)) ≤ ε.

A tower may increase L and decrease ε, but convergence to zero must be proved; it does not
follow merely from writing schedules Lk ↑ ∞ and εk ↓ 0.

11     Stochastic extension: what changes
For a stochastic transducer, a hidden state and input token determine a probability kernel
rather than one successor. Exact predictive equivalence should compare the joint law of
every ﬁnite future output trace under each ﬁxed future input word. To form a quotient
Markov process, one must also prove lumpability: states in one block assign equal transition
mass to every measurable quotient block, together with equality of output kernels (or the
appropriate joint kernel).
    These obligations require measurable spaces and kernels. Writing conditional probabili-
ties without specifying their measurable events, regular conditional distributions, and null-set
conventions is not a complete deﬁnition. For ﬁnite state and output spaces these issues re-
duce to ﬁnite sums; for general spaces they are substantive. The deterministic theorems in
this paper should therefore not be cited as proofs of the stochastic case.


12     Information measures are diagnostics, not certiﬁcates
For ﬁnite random variables, if a target U is exactly decodable from graph data Z and residual
data R, then H(U | Z, R) = 0 and the chain rule gives

                                H(U ) = I(U ; Z) + I(U ; R | Z).

If Zk = πk (Zk+1 ), data processing also gives I(U ; Zk ) ≤ I(U ; Zk+1 ) for a ﬁxed joint distri-
bution. These are useful accounting identities. They are distribution-dependent and do not
imply equality on zero-probability or adversarial words. Universal replacement still requires
a quotient, bisimulation, or direct trace certiﬁcate.
    For continuous variables, diﬀerential entropy may be negative or inﬁnite and the displayed
entropy identity needs ﬁniteness assumptions. It should not be used without stating them.


13     Examples and boundary cases
Parity. For alphabet {0, 1} and an observation reporting parity, the quotient has two states.
Token 0 preserves each state and token 1 swaps them. The four transition obligations and
two output obligations constitute a complete ﬁnite certiﬁcate.

Bounded context. A model whose state is exactly the last at most n tokens over a ﬁnite
vocabulary has a ﬁnite context graph. Behavioral minimization may merge contexts, but
similarity of sampled next-token distributions does not establish exact mergeability.

Unbounded counter. An integer counter incremented and decremented by tokens, with
an output testing zero, has inﬁnitely many future-distinguishable states: suitable repetitions
can bring one selected integer to zero while leaving another nonzero. No ﬁnite deterministic
exact quotient exists, although a register automaton or a bounded-horizon abstraction may
be appropriate.

Finite precision. A fully speciﬁed ﬁnite-memory hardware implementation has ﬁnitely
many machine states, so a ﬁnite quotient exists in principle. This says nothing about
tractable enumeration, graph size, or interpretability. The execution semantics must include
all state aﬀecting future outputs, such as caches, random seeds, and numerical modes.

14     Audit package
Each released level should include:

  1. model and execution-semantics identiﬁers;

  2. the reachable-state domain or its sound overapproximation;

  3. state encoding, graph transitions, outputs, and concretization information;

  4. the forgetful map to the preceding level and a check of (F);

  5. exact quotient or bisimulation obligations, with proof artifacts;

  6. for approximations, metric, horizon, quantiﬁers, and certiﬁed bound;

  7. transferred and residual execution domains, including routing exclusivity and coverage;

  8. an explicit status distinguishing proof from sampling.

A certiﬁcate hash can provide integrity, but a hash is not itself a correctness proof; it only
binds a result to speciﬁed artifacts.


15     Machine-checked core
The companion Lean ﬁle RequestProject/Spec.lean formalizes deterministic transducers
and ﬁnite-word execution. It proves:

  • transition congruence of future behavioral equivalence;

  • exact traces from a functional quotient certiﬁcate;

  • preservation of distinctions by a factorized reﬁnement;

  • trace equivalence from relational bisimulation;

  • extensional invariance of a certiﬁed patch.

The formalization is intentionally smaller than the prose framework: it certiﬁes the reusable
logical core and does not pretend to formalize probability kernels, neural ﬂoating-point se-
mantics, reachability solvers, or heuristic candidate generation.


16     Conclusion
A rigorous neural-to-graph extraction pipeline is built around commuting diagrams and
proof obligations, not visual similarity between hidden vectors. Coarse-to-ﬁne quotients are
coherent only when explicit forgetful maps factor their encodings. Exact graph execution
follows from functional quotient or bisimulation certiﬁcates. Residual draining is a separate
extensional replacement argument and permits parameter zeroing only after implementation-
level independence is established. Under ﬁnite enumerable semantics these steps terminate;
outside that setting, the result must carry either stronger eﬀective assumptions, a certiﬁed
approximation contract, or an explicit residual. This corrected separation of abstraction,
veriﬁcation, and deployment preserves the useful general idea while making its guarantees
auditable.
